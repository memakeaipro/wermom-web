The Best Breastfeeding Tracker Apps for 2026

By the Wermom Editorial Team · Evidence-checked against AAP & NHS guidance · Updated June 2026

Breastfeeding runs on details that are impossible to remember at 4 a.m.: which side you fed from last, how long the baby nursed, how the latch felt, and — if you pump — how much you got. A good breastfeeding tracker holds all of that so you can start the next feed on the right side and walk into a lactation visit with real data instead of a foggy guess. We compared the leading breastfeeding apps on the things that actually matter to a nursing parent: which-breast tracking, duration timers, latch notes, pumping logs, and how friendly they are in the dark. Here are the picks worth keeping.

Quick answer: For all-around breastfeeding tracking with which-breast, duration, latch notes, and pumping in one place, Wermom is our top pick. For pumping-first parents, Pump Log. For a simple free timer, Baby Tracker (Nighty). Trade-offs below.

1. Wermom — best all-around breastfeeding tracker (91/100)

Wermom gets the breastfeeding basics right: a one-tap last-side indicator so you always know where to start, a nursing timer that remembers which breast and for how long, a place for latch and supply notes, and pumping logs that sit alongside nursing rather than in a separate app. In our assessment it was the most night-feed-friendly of the group — large tap targets, dark-mode-friendly, no glare or hunting through menus. The lactation-ready export pulls feed frequency and pumping output into one summary. Skip if you exclusively pump and want deep pumping analytics only.

2. Pump Log — best for pumping-first parents (85/100)

Pump Log is built around the pumping session: output per side, timing, and trends over the day. If your milk story is mostly bottles and a pump, it goes deeper than a general tracker. Nursing-at-the-breast logging is lighter. Worth it if you are an exclusive or heavy pumper; less ideal if you mainly nurse directly.

3. Baby Daybook — detailed feed history (79/100)

Baby Daybook records nursing, bottles, and pumping in detail and keeps a thorough history you can scroll back through. It is more form-heavy than the fastest apps, which costs it in the middle-of-the-night moments. Worth a look if you value a complete archive over speed.

4. Baby Tracker (Nighty) — simple free timer (77/100)

For parents who just want to tap "left," start a timer, and move on, Baby Tracker is a clean, free option with the essentials and not much else. No pumping analytics, no export polish. Skip if you pump regularly or want lactation-ready summaries.

5. Glow Baby — stats-minded (74/100)

Glow Baby logs feeds and pumping and presents tidy summaries some parents enjoy, but ads and an account-heavy setup make it feel busier than a sleep-deprived nursing parent wants. Worth a look if you like granular numbers and don't mind the clutter.

What actually matters in a breastfeeding app

Across the apps we compared, four features separated the keepers from the deletions:

Which-breast / last-side memory. Knowing instantly which side to start on is the single most-used breastfeeding feature.
A reliable, resumable timer. Feeds get interrupted; the timer should survive a lock-screen and a fussy latch.
Pumping logs in the same place. Nursing and pumping belong together, not split across two apps.
Night-feed friendliness. Dark, large tap targets, and one-thumb operation are the difference between logging and giving up.

A note on safety and your lactation support

Breastfeeding apps help you notice patterns — they do not assess your milk supply or your baby's health. The American Academy of Pediatrics and the NHS both note that signs a baby is feeding well include frequent feeds and adequate wet and dirty diapers in the early weeks, which a tracker makes easy to record and share. If you have pain, latch trouble, supply worries, or your baby seems unsettled or is not gaining as expected, reach out to a lactation consultant, your pediatrician, or your health visitor — the app's job is to bring the data, not to replace that expert.
