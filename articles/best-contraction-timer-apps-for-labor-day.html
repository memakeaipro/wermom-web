The Best Contraction Timer Apps for Labor (2026)

By the Wermom Editorial Team · Evidence-checked against AAP & NHS guidance · Updated June 2026

When labor starts, you do not want to do mental math between contractions. A contraction timer app does one thing: you tap when a contraction begins and tap when it ends, and the app tracks how long each one lasts and how far apart they are. The best ones make that one tap unmissable, show your recent pattern at a glance, flag when you hit a common "time to call" threshold, and let you share a clean log with your partner or birth team. We compared the leading contraction timer apps on exactly those jobs. Here are the best for 2026 — and the safety framing that matters more than any app.

1. Full Term — best overall (89/100)

Full Term keeps it gloriously simple: a big start/stop button, a clean history of each contraction's duration and the interval between them, and an at-a-glance view of your recent pattern. It nods to the common 5-1-1 guide so you can see when contractions are getting closer, and you can share the log. Skip if you want extra bells and whistles — its strength is that it does one job, accurately, with almost no setup.

2. Contraction Timer (dedicated apps) — best simple shareable log (83/100)

Several apps named some version of "Contraction Timer" do the same core job well: tap to time intervals and duration, see averages, and export or share a tidy summary with your partner or birth team. The best have a large, mistake-proof button you can hit through a contraction. Skip if the one you pick is cluttered with ads or buries the timer — choose a cleaner alternative, since you need the button instantly.

3. Pregnancy+ contraction tool — best if you already use it (78/100)

If you already track your pregnancy in Pregnancy+, its built-in contraction timer saves you from installing anything new during labor. It times intervals and duration competently alongside the rest of your pregnancy data. Skip if you want the most focused experience — a dedicated single-purpose timer is faster to reach and harder to fumble than a tool tucked inside a larger app.

How to choose — and how to use one

Pick the app with the fastest, biggest one-tap button: during a contraction you should not have to hunt for it. Make sure it clearly shows both duration and interval, and that it can share or export a log so your partner can read out the numbers when you call your provider. Install and test it before your due date so you are not learning the interface in early labor. Then use it simply: tap at the start of a contraction, tap at the end, and let the app keep the running pattern. Resist the urge to time every single contraction for hours — most providers suggest timing for a stretch when the pattern changes, then resting.

The safety part that matters most

An app cannot tell you that you are in established labor, and it cannot decide when to go in — only your care team can. The popular 5-1-1 prompt (contractions roughly 5 minutes apart, each lasting about 1 minute, for at least 1 hour) is a general guide, and your own midwife or doctor may have given you different instructions based on your pregnancy, how far you live from the hospital, whether this is your first baby, or your medical history — always follow theirs over the app's. Call your maternity unit straight away, regardless of the timer, if your waters break, you have any bleeding, you notice reduced or changed baby movements, you have severe pain or a bad headache, or anything simply feels wrong. The NHS and ACOG both advise contacting your provider when you are unsure rather than waiting on a number.

FAQ

What is the best contraction timer app for labor in 2026? For accurate, no-frills timing of intervals and duration, Full Term (89/100) scored highest in our assessment; most dedicated Contraction Timer apps cover the same core job well, so pick the one with the clearest one-tap button.

How do contraction timer apps tell me when to go to the hospital? Many use the common 5-1-1 guide — contractions about 5 minutes apart, lasting about 1 minute, for at least 1 hour. Treat it as a general prompt only and follow the specific instructions your own midwife or doctor gave you.

Are contraction timer apps accurate? They are only as accurate as your taps — you start the timer when a contraction begins and stop it when it ends. They record intervals and duration reliably, but they cannot assess how your labor is actually progressing; your care team does that.
