The Best Pregnancy Tracker Apps for 2026

By the Wermom Editorial Team · Evidence-checked against AAP & NHS guidance · Updated June 2026

Pregnancy comes with a lot of questions and a lot of waiting between appointments, and the right app fills that gap with a week-by-week guide, a place to log symptoms, and reminders so nothing slips. A good pregnancy tracker should tell you what is happening this week in plain language, give you a kick counter and a symptom log you will actually keep up with, and help you walk into each prenatal visit organized rather than scrambling. We compared the most popular pregnancy apps on those jobs and ranked them for expecting parents who want signal, not anxiety. Here are the best pregnancy tracker apps for 2026.

Quick answer: For all-around week-by-week tracking with strong appointment and symptom logs, Ovia is our top pick. For beautiful development visuals, Pregnancy+. For supportive community and content, BabyCenter and What to Expect. If you tracked your cycle with Flo, its pregnancy mode is a smooth continuation. Details below.

1. Ovia Pregnancy — best all-around (88/100)

Ovia balances a clear week-by-week guide with the practical logging tools you will keep using: a kick counter, a flexible symptom log, weight and appointment tracking, and helpful reminders. The interface is clean and the data is easy to review before a check-up. Skip if you are privacy-cautious — read the data-sharing settings carefully, because the defaults are more open than some parents prefer.

2. Pregnancy+ — best development visuals (85/100)

Pregnancy+ is the prettiest week-by-week experience, with rich 3D-style development imagery and size comparisons that make each milestone feel real. The basics — kick counter, symptom notes, appointment list — are all there. Skip if you want deep logging or community; its strength is visuals and reassurance, not data depth.

3. BabyCenter — best content and community (80/100)

BabyCenter pairs a solid week-by-week guide with one of the largest parent communities and birth-month groups, which many find reassuring. The tracking tools are competent. Skip if ads bother you — the free experience is heavily monetized, and the content can feel buried.

4. What to Expect — best friendly guidance (78/100)

Built around the familiar pregnancy book, What to Expect offers warm, approachable week-by-week guidance plus active community groups. It is a comforting daily companion. Skip if you want advanced logging or charts; it leans more toward reading and reassurance than data tracking.

5. Flo — best for cycle-app users (74/100)

If you already tracked your cycle in Flo, its pregnancy mode is a seamless continuation with week-by-week updates and symptom logging in an interface you already know. Skip if you want a pregnancy-first design — pregnancy is one mode within a broader cycle app, so it is less specialized than dedicated pregnancy trackers.

How to choose the right pregnancy app

A few questions narrow it down fast. Do you want data or reassurance? If you like logging symptoms, weight, and appointments to review with your provider, Ovia leads; if you mainly want to see your baby's weekly development, Pregnancy+ wins. Do you want community? BabyCenter and What to Expect have the most active birth-month groups. Are you already in a cycle app? Flo's pregnancy mode is the path of least resistance. How much do you care about privacy? For any pregnancy app, open the data-sharing settings and turn off anything you are not comfortable with, since this is sensitive health information.

A note on kick counts and your provider

Pregnancy apps are organizers, not diagnostic tools. A kick counter is simply a convenient way to notice your baby's normal pattern of movement; it cannot tell you whether anything is wrong. The NHS and ACOG both stress that you should never wait on an app or "try later" if your baby's movements slow down, stop, or change — contact your midwife or maternity unit straight away, day or night. Likewise, use the symptom log to remember what to mention at your appointment, but bring anything that worries you (bleeding, severe headaches, vision changes, swelling, reduced movements) to your provider promptly rather than self-checking against an app.

FAQ

What is the best pregnancy tracker app for 2026? For week-by-week tracking with appointment and symptom logs, Ovia (88/100) scored highest in our assessment; Pregnancy+ (85/100) is best if you mainly want beautiful week-by-week development visuals.

Are pregnancy app kick counters accurate? A kick counter is just a convenient timer and tally; it does not diagnose anything. Use it to learn your baby's normal pattern, and contact your midwife or doctor right away if movements slow or change, rather than waiting on the app.

Are pregnancy tracker apps free? Most have a useful free tier covering week-by-week content and basic logging, with premium features behind a subscription. Pay only for something you will use weekly, such as detailed symptom logs or ad removal.
