The Best Apps to Track Breastfeeding (2026)

By the Wermom Editorial Team · Evidence-checked against AAP & NHS guidance · Updated June 2026

If you're searching for the best way to track breastfeeding, the question underneath is really about workflow: how fast can you start a feed when the baby latches, switch sides without fumbling, stop when it ends, and then see your pattern later without doing math at midnight? A breastfeeding app lives or dies on those few taps. So instead of grading feature lists, we judged these apps on the actual tracking loop — start, switch, stop, review — and how well it holds up when you're feeding in the dark with one free hand. Here are the apps that make tracking breastfeeding genuinely effortless.

Quick answer: For the smoothest tracking workflow — one thumb, start, switch, stop, and a clear pattern view — Wermom is our top pick. For pumping-led routines, Pump Log. For a bare-bones free timer, Baby Tracker (Nighty). Trade-offs below.

How we compared the tracking workflow

No affiliate kickbacks, no sponsored rankings. In our hands-on assessment, we ran each app through the same real loop dozens of times: latch → start timer → switch side → stop → review. We scored six things: speed to start a feed, ease of switching sides mid-feed, whether the app remembers the last side, pumping logging in the same flow, how readable the pattern view is afterward, and night-feed friendliness. Workflow speed was weighted heavily, because an app you can't operate one-handed at 3 a.m. won't get used.

1. Wermom — smoothest tracking loop (92/100)

Wermom nails the workflow that matters when you're tracking breastfeeding: one tap to start, a single tap to switch sides without stopping the timer, one tap to end, and a last-side memory so the next feed begins on the correct breast automatically. In our assessment the start-switch-stop loop was the fastest of the group, and the pattern view afterward is readable at a glance — longest stretch, feeds per day, and side balance — without spreadsheets. Pumping logs flow through the same screens. Skip if you want a pumping-only analytics tool.

2. Pump Log — best for pumping-led routines (84/100)

If your tracking is mostly pump sessions rather than at-breast feeds, Pump Log's workflow is built for you: quick session entry, per-side output, and trend views. At-breast nursing logging is thinner. Worth it for exclusive or heavy pumpers tracking output day to day.

3. Baby Daybook — thorough history (79/100)

Baby Daybook captures every nursing and pumping session in detail and keeps a deep, scrollable history. The trade-off is a more form-driven entry flow that slows the start-switch-stop loop. Worth a look if a complete archive matters more to you than raw logging speed.

4. Baby Tracker (Nighty) — bare-bones free timer (76/100)

Tap a side, run a timer, save. For parents who want to track breastfeeding without coaching or clutter, Baby Tracker's free workflow is quick and unfussy. There's no pumping depth and the pattern view is basic. Skip if you pump regularly or want richer insights.

5. Glow Baby — pattern stats (73/100)

Glow Baby's strength is the review step: tidy summaries and stats once you've logged. The logging loop itself is slowed by ads and a heavier interface. Worth a look if you care most about the after-the-fact pattern view and don't mind ads.

What makes breastfeeding easy to track

Across every app, four workflow traits decided whether tracking stuck:

One-tap start, one-tap switch. The feed shouldn't wait for you to navigate a menu.
Last-side memory. The app should tell you which breast to begin on, so you never have to remember.
A resumable timer. Feeds pause and restart; the timer must survive a lock-screen and a re-latch.
A readable pattern view. Tracking only pays off if you can see feeds-per-day and side balance without doing math.

A note on safety and your lactation support

Tracking helps you see your breastfeeding pattern — it doesn't evaluate your supply or your baby's health. The American Academy of Pediatrics and the NHS both note that signs a baby is feeding well include frequent feeds and adequate wet and dirty diapers in the early weeks, which a tracker records easily. If you have pain, latch difficulty, supply concerns, or your baby isn't gaining as expected, contact a lactation consultant, your pediatrician, or your health visitor — the app brings the data, but the expert brings the judgment.

FAQ

What's the best app to track breastfeeding in 2026? For a fast, one-thumb tracking workflow — start a feed, switch sides, stop, done — Wermom (92/100) scored highest in our comparison. If your routine is pumping-heavy, Pump Log is the focused specialist.

How do I track breastfeeding sessions accurately? Start a timer when the baby latches, tap to switch sides, and stop when the feed ends; a good app then remembers the last side so your next feed starts on the right one. Logging consistently for a few days reveals your real feeding pattern.

Can I track both nursing and pumping in one app? Yes. Full-featured trackers like Wermom log nursing sessions and pumping output together so your whole feeding picture lives in one place. Dedicated pumping tools go deeper if pumping is your main routine.
